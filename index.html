About

Driven by curiosity.
Grounded in science.

I am a neuroscience researcher at Binghamton University with a deep interest in addiction biology, sex differences, and the opioid system. My work sits at the intersection of behavioral neuroscience and translational medicine — using animal models to illuminate mechanisms that have real implications for human health.

My research focuses specifically on the enkephalin (ENK) / μ-opioid receptor (MOR) system in the ventral pallidum — a brain region critically involved in motivation, reward, and drug-seeking behavior. I am particularly committed to understanding why women face escalating vulnerability to substance use disorder, and how sex-specific neurobiology can inform better prevention and treatment.

Findings suggest potential for ENK/MOR-based biomarkers in the VP to identify high-risk individuals before SUD develops.

Sex-Specific Treatment

Results call for sex-tailored relapse prevention protocols targeting the hyperactive reward and withdrawal systems in females.

Sex Differences in Affective Behavior and the Augmentation of Enkephalin Expression in the Ventral Pallidum

An investigation into how biological sex shapes anxiety-like behavior, opioid consumption, and the enkephalin/MOR system in the ventral pallidum of Long-Evans rats.

Overview

The Big Picture

Substance use disorder (SUD) is one of the fastest-growing public health crises in the U.S. — and women are disproportionately bearing its rise. Yet neuroscience has historically studied addiction almost exclusively in males.

Background

The Science Behind the Study

Anxiety & SUD

In more than 50% of SUD cases, a preceding anxiety disorder was present. Children with anxiety disorders are significantly more likely to experiment with substances and develop SUD. Yet evidence on sex differences in anxiety remains mixed — some studies show females are less anxious, others show the opposite.

Women & Oxycodone

Women use ~8% more analgesics than men. Female rats consume 30–40% more oxycodone during escalation phases, show higher plasma oxycodone concentrations, and more readily reinstate drug-seeking behavior after extinction — pointing to heightened pharmacokinetic sensitivity and relapse vulnerability.

The Enkephalin / MOR System

Enkephalin (ENK) is the endogenous ligand for the μ-opioid receptor (MOR). When ENK binds to MORs on GABA interneurons, it inhibits GABA release — disinhibiting dopamine neurons and increasing dopamine signaling. This pathway mediates both the euphoric effects of opioids and withdrawal symptoms, making it a uniquely important target.

The Ventral Pallidum

The ventral pallidum (VP) is a key hub for motivated behavior and drug-seeking. As a major output of the nucleus accumbens, neural activity in the VP directly shapes behavioral outcomes. Despite known sex-specific differences in VP structure (e.g., vasopressin receptor expression), the ENK/MOR system within the VP had never been studied for sex differences.

01

Successive Alleys Test

Four linearly connected zones ranging from dark (anxiolytic) to light (anxiogenic). Rats placed in dark zone for 5 minutes. Anxiety measured by zone entries and time in Zone 1.

  • Entries into each alley
  • Time spent in each alley
  • Entries into Zone 1 (dark)
  • Total time in Zone 1
02

Oxycodone Self-Administration

Oral drinking paradigm. Rats given access to 0.06 mg/ml oxycodone solution for 3 hrs/day, 5 days/week, for 8 weeks. Consumption normalized to body weight.

  • Weekly average consumption
  • Eight-week average consumption
  • Total cumulative consumption
03

ENK ELISA

Competitive ELISA on VP tissue (LSBio Rat ENK ELISA Kit, LS-F39250). ENK in samples competes with pre-coated antigen to bind detection antibody. OD measured at 450 nm — lower OD = higher ENK.

  • Standard dilution series (0–40 ng/mL)
  • HRP-TMB color development
  • 96-well microplate reader
Results

What We Found

H1

Anxiety-Like Behavior

Not Supported

No significant difference was found between male and female rats on any of the four anxiety measures:

  • Alley entries: F(1,60) = 0.308, p = .580
  • Time per alley: F(1,240) = 0.004, p = .948
  • Zone 1 entries: t(60) = 0.08, p = .930
  • Time in Zone 1: t(60) = −0.338, p = .736

Supported — p < .001

H3

Enkephalin Expression in the Ventral Pallidum

Supported — p = .032

Female rats expressed significantly higher ENK levels in the VP:

  • Male ENK: 59.62 ± 34.71 pg/mL
  • Female ENK: 85.15 ± 21.59 pg/mL
  • Test: t(16.71) = 1.97, p = .032

This is the first reported sex difference in pallidal ENK expression. The higher ENK in females may reflect augmented MOR activity — possibly through desensitization — suggesting a more active reward and withdrawal system even at baseline.

Discussion

What It Means

Why no anxiety difference?

The absence of sex differences in anxiety-like behavior in a non-stressed environment is consistent with prior work (Yang et al., 2019; Vaughan et al., 2025). Sex differences in anxiety may be context-dependent — emerging under stress conditions rather than in baseline environments. The specific test used may also matter: Scholl et al. (2019) found inconsistent sex differences across different anxiety tests.

Females consume more — why?

Female rats consumed significantly more oxycodone at every time point. This aligns with the broader literature (Doyle et al., 2023; Kimbrough et al., 2020) and may reflect sex-specific pharmacokinetic processing, hormonal modulation of opioid sensitivity, and greater reinstatement after extinction. The pattern mirrors the clinical rise in female opioid use disorder.

What does elevated ENK mean?

Higher ENK in females suggests greater activity in the reward and withdrawal systems — even without opioid exposure. Elevated ENK can lead to MOR desensitization (Gupta, 2021), which may prime females for heightened opioid sensitivity and relapse risk. This neurobiological profile could precede and predict SUD vulnerability.

The VP as a missing piece

The ventral pallidum, as a key output of the nucleus accumbens, shapes motivational behavior. Its near-total absence from sex-difference research in addiction is striking. Our data provide the first evidence that sex differences in the ENK/MOR system exist in this region — opening a new frontier for targeted intervention.

Limitations

  • Only one anxiety test (Successive Alleys) was used; results may differ across tests
  • ELISA detection limits prevented direct MOR quantification in the VP
  • ENK was measured prior to oxycodone exposure — future studies should assess neuroadaptations after consumption
Implications

Where This Goes

⊙

Biomarker-Based Early Detection

ENK/MOR-related biomarkers in the VP, potentially measurable via PET imaging, could identify individuals — especially women — at high risk for SUD before it develops. This represents a paradigm shift from treatment to prevention.

⊕

Sex-Specific Relapse Prevention

The hyperactive reward/withdrawal system in females, reflected in elevated ENK, suggests females may benefit from targeted pharmacotherapy that normalizes MOR sensitivity. Current one-size-fits-all protocols leave females underserved.

⊗

Future Research Directions

Direct measurement of MOR expression and function, assessment of estradiol modulation of ENK/MOR activity, comparison of anxiety-like behavior with and without stressors, and clinical translation to human trials are all urgent next steps.

"The strong sex-specific differences observed in this study call our attention to females' heightened vulnerability to SUD. Identifying ENK/MOR-related biomarkers in the VP could allow prevention before SUD emerges."
